# Airborne rice disease forecast

Predicts, day by day for up to 14 days, the outbreak risk of the two airborne or
splash-dispersed diseases the leaf scanner recognizes: **BLB** (bacterial leaf blight)
and **BLAST** (rice blast). For an existing hotspot it also predicts
**which direction the outbreak is likely to spread**.

Each disease has its own **Random Forest** (scikit-learn) and **XGBoost** model. A soft
vote blends the two, weighted by walk-forward validation. All models are trained on
**five years of hourly Open-Meteo weather** (ERA5 reanalysis) from 12 rice-growing points
across Davao del Norte and western Davao de Oro.

## Pipeline

| Step | Module | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. History | `analytics/ml/sources.py` | Downloads 5 years of hourly weather per location from the Open-Meteo archive (free, no key). Caches one CSV per location-year under `datasets/weather/hourly/`. |
| 2. Daily table | `sources.to_daily` | Hourly to daily statistics: temperature, humidity, dew point, vapour-pressure deficit, rain, vector-averaged wind, radiation, reference evaporation. Training and live inference use the **same** function. |
| 3. Labels | `analytics/ml/epidemiology.py` | Process-based infection model run over the **hourly** record (see below). |
| 4. Features | `analytics/ml/features.py` | 195 inputs from the daily table only: same-day weather, 3 to 60-day rolling windows, lags, streaks, agronomic indicator days, crop water balance, season harmonics, location. |
| 5. Training | `analytics/ml/train.py` | Time-based split. Walk-forward validation sets the ensemble weights and decision threshold. The final year is held out for scoring, then the model is refit on all 5 years. |
| 6. Inference | `analytics/ml/predict.py` | Pulls 75 observed days plus the forecast from Open-Meteo, builds the same features, and returns probabilities, risk bands and spread direction. |

## Where the training labels come from

There is no five-year record of confirmed field outbreaks for this area. The app's own
scan history only starts when the app went live. The labels are therefore generated by
an epidemiological infection model run over the real hourly weather, the usual approach
for weather-driven plant-disease early warning when field records are thin.

- **Blast** needs long continuous leaf wetness at mild temperatures (best around
  24 deg C). Cool dewy nights favour it, and heavy rain washes spores off.
- **BLB** follows warm humid weather and, above all, wind-driven rain and storms, which
  spread bacteria into leaf wounds.
- Each disease has a latency period (about 6 to 10 days), secondary infection from
  existing lesions, decay, crop-stage susceptibility over two cropping seasons a year,
  and random noise for inoculum arrival and scouting error.

The label model uses hourly detail, such as how many consecutive hours leaves stayed
wet and how warm it was during those hours. **The classifier never sees it.** The
classifier only gets daily summaries, the same kind of data a daily forecast provides.
It also has to predict an outcome shaped by latency and weeks of accumulated disease
pressure. That is why the scores below are well short of perfect.

Rate constants were calibrated so each disease reaches outbreak level on roughly 12% of
days, in about one month-long episode per location per year, with real year-to-year
variation.

## Results on the held-out final year

Scored on the last 365 days (Sept 2025 to Sept 2026), which training never saw. A
21-day embargo separates it from the training data. The baseline is the app's current
rule in `analytics/weather.py`: "humidity at or above the disease's threshold means the
disease is spreading".

| Disease | Model | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | F1 | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLB | RF + XGBoost ensemble | 0.83 | 0.32 | 0.38 | 0.30 | 0.50 |
| BLB | current humidity rule | 0.45 | 0.07 | 0.14 | 0.08 | 0.95 |
| Blast | RF + XGBoost ensemble | 0.87 | 0.47 | 0.51 | 0.40 | 0.71 |
| Blast | current humidity rule | 0.56 | 0.09 | 0.14 | 0.10 | 0.22 |

Outbreak days made up about 7% of the test year, so PR-AUC and F1 are the honest
measures; accuracy would flatter any model. The current humidity rule does no better
than chance. Humidity is high nearly every day in this climate, so on its own it cannot
separate outbreak days from ordinary ones.

The strongest blast signals are 30 to 60-day averages of humidity and vapour-pressure
deficit. For BLB they are the season harmonics and the prevailing wind over two to three
weeks. Full metrics, confusion matrices and top features are in
`ai_models/disease_forecast/metadata.json`.

## Commands

Run from `oryzawatch_backend/` with the repo-root virtualenv:

```powershell
# Install (once)
uv pip install --python ..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scikit-learn xgboost pandas joblib

# Download 5 years of history (about 4 min the first time, cached after), build the table, train (about 2 min)
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py train_disease_forecast

# Retrain from the cached table without re-downloading
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py train_disease_forecast --use-cache

# Hyperparameter search: slower, sometimes a little better
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py train_disease_forecast --use-cache --tune

# Forecasts from the command line
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py predict_disease_risk --municipality CARMEN
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py predict_disease_risk --lat 7.40 --lng 125.65 --days 10
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py predict_disease_risk --hotspot 5
```

Retrain every few months with `train_disease_forecast` (no `--use-cache`), so the newest
season is included. The server picks up new model files automatically, with no restart.
Model files are gitignored like the leaf-scanner models, so every deployment has to
train or copy in `ai_models/disease_forecast/`. Until then the endpoints return 503.

## API

Both endpoints require a logged-in user. They share a `disease_forecast` rate limit of
20 requests per minute and cache results for 30 minutes per ~1 km cell. They only accept
points inside the service area (roughly Mindanao), since the model knows nothing about
other climates.

### `GET /api/analytics/disease-risk/`

| Query | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `lat`, `lng` | A specific point |
| `municipality` | `CARMEN` or `ASUNCION` town centre |
| *(neither)* | The requesting farmer's registered farm |
| `days` | 1 to 14, default 7 |

```json
{
  "source": "municipality:CARMEN",
  "forecast_days": 7,
  "latitude": 7.36, "longitude": 125.7,
  "days": [
    {
      "date": "2026-09-23",
      "weather": {"t_min": 24.1, "t_max": 31.8, "rh_mean": 90, "precip_mm": 4.1,
                  "wind_from": "NW", "spread_toward": "SE", "wind_steadiness": 0.32, "...": "..."},
      "risks": {
        "BLB":   {"name": "Bacterial Leaf Blight", "probability": 0.11, "level": "MODERATE"},
        "BLAST": {"name": "Rice Blast",            "probability": 0.10, "level": "LOW"}
      },
      "dominant": "BLB"
    }
  ],
  "peak": {"BLB":   {"date": "2026-09-23", "probability": 0.11, "level": "MODERATE"},
           "BLAST": {"date": "2026-09-22", "probability": 0.11, "level": "MODERATE"}},
  "model": {"trained_at": "...", "algorithms": ["..."], "history": "2021-11-10 to 2026-09-10"}
}
```

Risk levels are relative to each disease's tuned decision threshold, because the
diseases have different base rates. `HIGH` means the model would call an outbreak
(probability at or above the threshold). `SEVERE` is 1.6 times the threshold or more,
`MODERATE` is 0.55 times or more, and anything lower is `LOW`.

### `GET /api/analytics/hotspots/<id>/spread-forecast/?days=7`

Answers "where is this outbreak going?" for the hotspot's detected disease. Risk is
forecast at the hotspot and at 16 ring points, 10 and 20 km out in the 8 compass
directions, in a single weather call. Each day, every ring point gets a spread score:

```
source probability x destination probability x downwind alignment
```

Alignment is the cosine of the angle between the point and that day's downwind heading.
It is zero beyond 45 degrees, and zero on days when the wind swung around too much to
trust. The response gives `likely_direction` and `likely_destination` per day, plus every
ring point with its probability, risk level and `downwind` flag, ready to draw on the map.

Open-Meteo reports wind as the direction it blows **from**. The spread forecast turns
that around (`spread_toward`), because spores travel downwind.

## Limits and next steps

- **The labels are simulated, not observed.** The scores above show how well the model
  recovers the infection process from daily weather. They are not field accuracy.
  Before farmers rely on it, compare its HIGH and SEVERE days with confirmed scans and
  hotspots. The existing `ForecastPrediction` model already has verified-outcome fields
  that these forecasts could be logged against.
- **Real reports are the biggest upgrade available.** Weather alone cannot tell whether
  disease is already present nearby, which is the main source of error. Once confirmed
  scans cover a season or two, add "confirmed cases within N km in the last 14 days" as
  a feature and blend real outcomes into the labels.
- **The crop calendar is assumed.** Transplanting is modelled around early June and
  early December, give or take about 12 days. The season harmonics rank among the top
  features, so if local planting shifts, update `PLANTING_DOY` in `epidemiology.py`
  and retrain.
- **Weather grid resolution is about 9 to 25 km.** Two farms in the same grid cell get
  the same weather, and so the same risk.
- **Forecast skill fades after about 7 days.** Days 8 to 14 inherit that uncertainty.
- The saved models are joblib pickles. They are loaded only from the fixed
  `ai_models/disease_forecast/` directory and never from anything a user uploads.

## Station export training (13 October 2026)

The semicolon-delimited weather exports can be imported with:

```powershell
..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe manage.py train_station_forecast --directory "C:\Users\Rivan\Downloads\WeatherDataset\Datasets (Requerme, Ivan)" --jobs 4
```

Use `--prepare-only` to audit and build data without training, and `--use-prepared`
to retrain the prepared table. This command runs offline without a database connection.
It writes `datasets/weather/station_exports/training.csv` and `data_audit.json`, and
saves both disease bundles and evaluation metadata under
`ai_models/disease_forecast/station_candidate/`. It does not replace active bundles.

The initial run audited 51 files. Davao PAGASA and Carmen Ising contributed 3,532
hybrid location-days. The other 49 files lacked full weather variables or sufficient
continuous history. Cached Open-Meteo history within 1 km fills missing station
measurements, with the measured and substituted hour counts recorded per variable.
For example, Davao temperature observations covered 30,327 of 43,824 cached hours;
Carmen covered 3,629 of 43,824. These are supplemented station records, not entirely
measured weather histories. Unsupported stations are split at gaps rather than
interpolated. Original source files are unchanged and SHA-256 hashes are recorded.

Hourly aggregation requires 80% coverage at the inferred observation interval.
Minute rainfall is summed, overlapping one-hour rain totals are excluded, m/s winds
are converted to km/h, and wind directions use circular averages. Dew point is
derived from temperature and humidity. Rainfall interval semantics and sensor heights
still require provider confirmation. Live prediction inputs remain Open-Meteo.

The combined table contains 22,958 location-days across 13 locations, from 2 March
2021 to 10 September 2026. Hybrid Carmen rows replace overlapping regional Carmen
rows, avoiding duplicate examples. Training retains the existing 195 predictors,
walk-forward validation, final 365-day holdout, and 21-day embargo.

| Candidate | Held-out F1 | Held-out average precision |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bacterial Leaf Blight | 0.365 | 0.309 |
| Rice Blast | 0.503 | 0.472 |

These scores measure recovery of simulated disease labels, not accuracy against
confirmed outbreaks. The evaluation population differs from the previous training
run, so the scores do not establish that the candidates improve on active models.
The candidates were retained for evaluation; active models were preserved.

No network access or trained model needed. The tests cover label-free features,
vector-averaged wind, label determinism, the fold embargo, feature-order safety, input
validation, and the API's 200, 400, 401, 503, caching and hotspot paths.
